# Seller Handover Performance and Delivery Delays

## Objective

Investigate what is the role or sellers in late deliveries.

## Methodology

- **Period:** January 2017 – August 2018.
- Only delivered orders with available shipping and delivery dates.
- Only single-seller orders, to make sure each order can be linked to one seller.
- **Late handoff:** The order was handed over to the carrier after the shipping deadline.
- **Late delivery:** The customer received the order after the estimated delivery date.
- Seller comparisons include only sellers with at least 100 orders.

## Key Findings

### Seller performance

Late-handover rates differed quite a lot between sellers.

- Seller 1904 had the highest late-handover rate among the sellers included in the comparison: **45.73% across 234 orders**.
- Seller 1183 had **189 late handoffs out of 971 orders (19.46%)**. Even with a lower percentage, this represents a relatively large number of affected orders.
- High late-handover rates didn't always mean high late-delivery rates. For example, seller 2364 handed over **32.08%** of orders late, but only **5.66%** were delivered late to customers.

This shows that some sellers miss their shipping deadlines much more often than others. However, late handoffs don't always result in late deliveries.

### Late handoffs and deliveries

- **26.9%** of orders handed over late were also delivered late, compared with **5.6%** of orders handed over on time.
- Orders handed over late were about **4.8 times more likely** to arrive late.
- However, around **78% of late deliveries** occurred even though the order was handed over to the carrier on time.

Late handoffs are associated with a much higher chance of late delivery. At the same time, most late deliveries happened after an on-time handoff, so seller performance is only part of the picture.

## Recommendations

Track late-handover rates for individual sellers, using these initial thresholds:

- **Below 5%:** Target.
- **5–10%:** Monitor.
- **Above 10%:** Investigate.

Apply these thresholds to sellers with at least 100 orders.

Sellers with both high late-handover rates and large order volumes should be reviewed first, since improvements there could affect more orders.

These thresholds are suggested starting points, not industry standards. They can be adjusted based on future results.

## Limitations

The analysis shows an association between late handoffs and late deliveries, but doesn't prove that one causes the other.

Delivery times can also be affected by carriers, transportation distances, and other factors that weren't included in the analysis.

The results are based on historical Olist data and don't provide enough information to determine responsibility for individual delivery delays.


## Additional Findings

### Customer Retention

Only **2.25% of customers made purchases on more than one date**, despite generally positive customer reviews (average score of 4.08/5) and a relatively low late-delivery rate of 6.7%.

This raises an interesting question about customer retention, although the available data doesn't explain why customers don't return.

The result should be treated with caution. The query counts purchases on different dates rather than individual orders, and the dataset covers a limited period. Customers who made their next purchase after the end of the dataset wouldn't be counted.

### Delivery Times and Expectations

The average delivery took **12.56 days**, while the average estimated delivery time was **23.77 days**. Only **6.7% of delivered orders** arrived after their estimated delivery date.

However, around **70% of deliveries took more than a week**.

Orders spent an average of **2.8 days between approval and carrier handoff**, followed by **9.33 days in transit**.

This suggests that delivery estimates were relatively generous. Most orders arrived within the promised timeframe, but delivery itself wasn't particularly fast.

It also shows why looking only at the late-delivery rate might not give a complete picture of delivery performance.
